**In short:** a property that can become a designated driving school is decided mainly by three things — (1) the zoning district (a driving school can be built up to 3,000 sqm of floor area in a Category I Residential Zone, without limit from a Category II Residential Zone through the Industrial Exclusive Zone, and not at all in the low-rise and mid-high-rise residential-exclusive zones or the agricultural-residential zone; Table 2 of the Building Standards Act); (2) the size of the course (as a rule 8,000 sqm or more under the Cabinet Office Ordinance on Designated Driving Schools, or 3,500 sqm for a school that handles only large/ordinary motorcycle licences); and (3) whether the building can hold classrooms, a waiting area, a rest room and toilets. You can form a view at the property stage, but the final decision on designation rests with the Prefectural Public Safety Commission.

Unlike a care-service office or a clinic, a driving-school property does not stumble on "the zoning district." It stumbles on whether **a large piece of land for the course, plus a building for classrooms and the skills-training waiting/rest rooms, can all be secured on one site.** "We found the building but the land for the course fell short"; "there was land, but no floor for a classroom" — notice these after signing and you redo the whole design. This is for those who want to open a new designated driving school (or a notified driving school for paper-licence refresher lessons) and are at the property-search stage, setting out what you can check before signing, from the Road Traffic Act, the Building Standards Act and the Cabinet Office Ordinance. The final check on whether it can be done is at the Prefectural Public Safety Commission (the driver's-licence section of the Prefectural Police Headquarters) with jurisdiction over the location.

## Which zoning districts allow a designated driving school to be built?

A driving school is a building subject to zoning-district restrictions under the Building Standards Act. **It cannot be built in the low-rise or mid-high-rise residential-exclusive zones or the agricultural-residential zone; it can be built up to 3,000 sqm of floor area in a Category I Residential Zone, and without limit from a Category II Residential Zone through the Industrial Exclusive Zone.**

Under Article 48 and Table 2 of the Building Standards Act (Act No. 201 of 1950), whether a driving school can be built depends on the zoning district. Because the course land is large and buildings (a classroom block, a skills-training waiting area, garages) come with it, confirming the zoning district is the starting point for choosing land. Per the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism's summary of use restrictions, it is roughly as follows.

| Zoning district | Driving school |
|---|---|
| Category I/II low-rise residential-exclusive, agricultural-residential | Cannot be built |
| Category I/II mid-high-rise residential-exclusive | Cannot be built |
| Category I Residential | Allowed up to 3,000 sqm of floor area |
| Category II Residential, Quasi-residential | Allowed without limit |
| Neighbourhood commercial, Commercial | Allowed without limit |
| Quasi-industrial, Industrial, Industrial exclusive | Allowed without limit |

Because the course land is large, Category II Residential / Quasi-residential and the quasi-industrial / industrial zones — where sizeable plots come up more readily — tend to be candidates, while in a Category I Residential Zone you must watch the 3,000 sqm cap on the building portion. In an urbanisation-control area the question of development permission (Article 29 of the City Planning Act) is layered on, so confirm the area classification of the site first. ## How large and what shape must the course (the driving track) be?

The course of a designated driving school needs **as a rule 8,000 sqm or more** (3,500 sqm or more for a school that handles only large/ordinary motorcycle skills-training and skills-testing). On top of that, tracks such as a circuit, a trunk road, a slope, a bend, a curve and a turning area must fit together on one contiguous piece of land.

The physical standards for a designated driving school are set, under Article 99, paragraph 1 of the Road Traffic Act, in the Cabinet Office Ordinance on Designated Driving Schools. The Ordinance puts the area of the course (the track used for skills-training and skills-testing) at **as a rule 8,000 sqm or more** (3,500 sqm or more for a school that conducts only large/ordinary motorcycle-licence skills-training and skills-testing) and specifies the types of track needed for ordinary-licence training. The key points when looking at land (the course) are as follows.

| Where to look | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Course area | Whether 8,000 sqm or more (3,500 sqm for motorcycle-only) can be secured as one contiguous piece of land. Green areas and road shoulders count, but the land for classroom buildings is considered separately |
| Course shape | A shape on which a circuit, trunk road, slope, bend, curve and turning tracks can be laid out without strain. Hard on long-thin or divided plots |
| Entrance and the front road | A frontage and road width that let training cars and test vehicles come and go without obstruction. Whether noise and traffic flow clash with neighbours |
| Gradient and earthworks | Whether a usable gradient for the slope course can be formed; whether earthworks and drainage costs pencil out |

Because the figures and the details of the tracks are fixed by the Ordinance's standards and each Prefectural Public Safety Commission's review criteria and practice, at the land stage it is practical to **first see "whether the required tracks can be laid out on one contiguous 8,000 sqm (3,500 sqm for motorcycles) site"**. Confirm the specific standards with the Public Safety Commission (Prefectural Police Headquarters) with jurisdiction.

## What floor area and equipment do the classroom and the skills-training waiting/rest rooms need?

For classrooms, the law speaks of "an appropriate size and number according to the number of people receiving instruction," and **no uniform floor area (how many sqm per person) is fixed in law.** Alongside ancillary facilities such as a waiting area, a rest room and toilets, you look at whether the building side can secure them.

The Cabinet Office Ordinance on Designated Driving Schools requires that classrooms for lecture-based instruction be "of an appropriate size and number according to the number of people receiving instruction," so the requirement is to secure space and a number of rooms commensurate with the lecture attendance. Accordingly, **this article does not state a specific figure for the per-person floor area of a classroom** (because the law fixes no uniform figure). The specific floor area and number of rooms are judged by each Prefectural Public Safety Commission's review criteria and practice. What you can confirm when looking at a building is the following.

| Where to look | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Classroom | Whether space and a number of rooms commensurate with the assumed attendance per session can be secured; whether lighting, ventilation, air-conditioning and audiovisual equipment can be installed |
| Skills-training waiting/rest room | Whether a room where trainees can wait and rest can be secured |
| Office / reception | Whether a room for reception, car dispatch and certificate issuance can be secured |
| Toilets / ancillary facilities | Whether toilets and other ancillary facilities commensurate with attendance can be provided |

Because the specific area and number of rooms turn on the Public Safety Commission's review criteria, at the building stage it is safe to **see "whether classrooms, a waiting area, an office and toilets commensurate with the assumed attendance can be laid out without strain," and confirm the appropriateness of the figures with the Public Safety Commission.** If you lease, agree in writing before signing on the use (driving school), the fit-out and the scope of restoration. Which parts of a lease to read is set out in [Which parts of a lease agreement should you read?](https://luck428.com/column/chintaishaku-keiyakusho-doko-wo-yomu).

## How does the way you view a property differ between a notified driving school (paper-licence lessons) and a designated school?

Because a notified driving school has no system of skills-test exemption via skills-testing and a graduation certificate, **it is not uniformly required to meet the course-area physical standard (8,000 sqm) of a designated school.** On the other hand, if you aim for a designated school, you must choose from the outset a property that can hold both the course land and the classroom/waiting/rest rooms.

The Road Traffic Act distinguishes a "notified driving school" (Article 98, paragraph 2), whose operator notifies the Public Safety Commission, from a "designated driving school" (Article 99, paragraph 1), which the Public Safety Commission designates from among notified schools meeting the standards for staff, equipment and so on. Once designated, a graduate who passes the skills test is exempted from the skills test at the driver's-licence testing centre (Article 99-2). A notified school offering, say, paper-licence refresher lessons has no such skills-test exemption, so the level required of the property differs.

| Category | Basis | What it can do | How to view the property/land |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notified driving school | Art. 98, para. 2 (notification to the Public Safety Commission) | Instruction/lessons (such as paper-licence refresher lessons). No skills-test exemption | No uniform course-area standard is required. Whether a place and equipment usable for lessons can be secured |
| Designated driving school | Art. 99, para. 1 (designation by the Public Safety Commission) | Plus skills-test exemption via skills-testing and a graduation certificate (Art. 99-2) | Secure a course of as a rule 8,000 sqm or more, plus classrooms, a waiting area, a rest room, an office and toilets, together |

So if you aim to be designated later, **even if you start as a notified school and switch to designation afterwards, keeping in view from the start land to which the course can be added and a building that can serve as a classroom** cuts down on rework. For both notification and designation, the standards for equipment and instruction rest on the Public Safety Commission's judgment.

## Who do you ask to confirm, before signing, whether designation is possible?

The property (real estate), the requirements judgment and application to the Public Safety Commission for designation/notification, building confirmation and use fit, the labour matters of instructors and examiners, and tax — the parties differ. Whether designation itself is possible is judged by the Public Safety Commission.

Designation of a designated driving school is reviewed not only on physical standards such as the course and classrooms but also on human standards — a manager, skills examiners and driving instructors (the items of Article 99, paragraph 1). **If you fix the property first, you can end up redoing it because the course area or the classroom/waiting space falls short** — which is exactly why the above points are set out before signing. ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. What is the minimum area for the course of a designated driving school?**
A. The Cabinet Office Ordinance on Designated Driving Schools puts the area of the course (the track used for skills-training and skills-testing) at as a rule 8,000 sqm or more (3,500 sqm or more for a school that conducts only large/ordinary motorcycle-licence skills-training and skills-testing). Green areas and road shoulders count toward the area, but the land for buildings such as classrooms is considered separately. Whether the tracks (circuit, trunk road, slope, bend, curve, turning area, etc.) can be laid out on one contiguous piece of land without strain also matters. Confirm the specific standards and practice with the Prefectural Public Safety Commission with jurisdiction.

**Q. Is a classroom fixed at so many sqm per person?**
A. The law does not fix the per-person floor area of a classroom at a uniform figure. The Cabinet Office Ordinance on Designated Driving Schools requires classrooms for lecture-based instruction to be "of an appropriate size and number according to the number of people receiving instruction." The point, then, is whether space and a number of rooms commensurate with the assumed attendance can be secured, and the specific area and number of rooms are judged by each Prefectural Public Safety Commission's review criteria and practice. Confirm the appropriateness of the figures with the Public Safety Commission.

**Q. In which zoning districts can a driving school be built?**
A. Under Table 2 of the Building Standards Act, a driving school can be built up to 3,000 sqm of floor area in a Category I Residential Zone, and without limit in the Category II Residential, Quasi-residential, Neighbourhood Commercial, Commercial, Quasi-industrial, Industrial and Industrial Exclusive zones. It cannot be built in the Category I/II low-rise residential-exclusive zones, the Category I/II mid-high-rise residential-exclusive zones, or the agricultural-residential zone. In an urbanisation-control area the question of development permission (Article 29 of the City Planning Act) is layered on, so confirm the area classification of the site first.

**Q. If it is only paper-licence refresher lessons, is the 8,000 sqm course not needed?**
A. A "notified driving school" (Article 98, paragraph 2 of the Road Traffic Act) offering paper-licence refresher lessons and the like has no system of skills-test exemption via skills-testing and a graduation certificate, so it is not required to meet the uniform course-area standard of a designated driving school. That said, if you aim for "designation" later, choosing from the outset a property that can hold both the course land and the classroom/waiting/rest rooms cuts down on rework. For both notification and designation, the standards for equipment and instruction rest on the Public Safety Commission's judgment.
