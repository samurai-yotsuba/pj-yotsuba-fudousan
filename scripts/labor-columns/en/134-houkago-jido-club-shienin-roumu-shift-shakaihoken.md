**Bottom line first**: The labour of an after-school children's club (gakudo) turns on the shifts that keep two or more after-school child support staff per support unit (Standards on Equipment and Operation of After-School Child Health Development Services = Ordinance of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare No. 63 of 2014, Article 10), the duty design for the long opening hours from weekday afternoon into the evening and across the long holidays, and part-time support staff's social insurance. The standard on the number and qualification of support staff changed from a national "standard to comply with" to a "standard to refer to" on 1 April 2020 (Reiwa 2), so the applicable standard is set by the municipality's ordinance. The notification and operating standards belong to a Gyoseishoshi, the property and use district to a real-estate agent, and tax to a tax accountant, each contracted separately as an independent business entity. It is a different system from after-school day services (ambulatory support for children with disabilities).

This page sets out not the staffing standard itself, but the design of opening hours, shifts and social insurance needed to keep meeting it. The notification to the municipality and conformity with the operating standards are the area of a Gyoseishoshi and the municipality; this article focuses on labour.

## How many after-school child support staff are needed per support unit, and how does that affect labour?

Article 10 of the Standards on Equipment and Operation of After-School Child Health Development Services (Ordinance of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare No. 63 of 2014) sets the number of after-school child support staff at two or more per support unit. However, all but one of them may be replaced by an assistant (a person who assists the support the after-school child support staff provide).

| Item | Content (Ordinance No. 63 of 2014, Article 10) |
|---|---|
| Number of support staff | Two or more after-school child support staff per support unit; all but one may be replaced by an assistant |
| Support unit | Composed of approximately 40 or fewer children (Article 10(4)) |
| Support-staff qualification | A person who holds a basic qualification such as nursery teacher or certified social worker and has completed training conducted by the prefectural governor etc. (Article 10(3)) |

What labour must grasp is that **"two or more" is a placement requiring two to be on-site at all times, so you must build shifts that do not drop below it throughout the opening hours**. Planning the headcount including assistants and designing the roster so the unit never drops below two even when a support worker is on paid leave or sick leave are labour tasks. The standard on the number and qualification of support staff was revised from the national "standard to comply with" to a "standard to refer to" on 1 April 2020 (Reiwa 2), and the applicable standard is set by the municipality's ordinance. Check how your municipality's ordinance sets it with the municipality you notify. The duty to prepare work rules is set out in [From how many employees are work rules mandatory](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## What shifts work for the long opening hours of weekday afternoon-to-evening and the long holidays?

An after-school children's club opens from after school into the evening on weekdays, and from morning into the evening on Saturdays and long holidays such as summer. One support worker's scheduled hours (8 hours a day) cannot cover a day's opening, so you build a shift system on rotation that always secures two or more per support unit.

| Opening type | Labour consideration |
|---|---|
| Weekday (after school to evening) | Even if opening is short, a time band requiring two or more arises. Fill the bands with a combination of part-time support staff |
| Long holiday (morning to evening) | A day's opening is long and exceeds one worker's scheduled hours. Use early/late rotation, securing two or more in overlapping bands too |
| Saturday / school-closure day | The number of children and staff differs from weekdays, so prepare a separate roster |

What matters here is a roster combining early shifts, late shifts and part-timers. Because opening hours differ greatly between weekdays and long holidays, there are peaks across the year. Working-hour management and roster design directly govern conformity with the staffing standard.

## Can you use variable working hours or the yearly variable system?

Because the allotment of working hours differs greatly between weekdays and long holidays, a variable working-hours system is an option.

| System | Basis | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly variable working hours | Labour Standards Act Article 32-2 | When duty concentrates on Saturdays and school-closure days within a month. Set in work rules or a labour-management agreement |
| Yearly variable working hours | Labour Standards Act Article 32-4 | When duty concentrates in long holidays such as summer, with peaks across a year. A labour-management agreement and filing are required |

If you adopt yearly variable working hours, even a worksite with fewer than 10 regular workers cannot use the 44-hour-a-week special rule and calculates on 40 hours a week, with limits on working days, consecutive working days and daily/weekly hours over the target period. Work beyond statutory hours requires a 36 Agreement and premium wages (Labour Standards Act Article 37). Choosing a system is set out in [Choosing variable working hours and flextime](/en/labor/column/henkei-roudoujikan-flextime-erabikata), and the overtime cap in [How far can a 36 Agreement allow overtime](/en/labor/column/36-kyotei-zangyo-sharoushi-doko-made). Which system fits your opening pattern turns on the number of long-holiday days and how the roster is built.

## When do part-time and short-hour support staff need social insurance?

Even a part-time after-school child support worker or assistant becomes an insured person under health insurance and employees' pension insurance if their weekly scheduled working hours and monthly scheduled working days are three-quarters or more of a regular worker at the same establishment. A short-hour worker below this three-quarters still joins if they meet the requirements of the expanded coverage for short-hour workers.

| Category | Guide to joining |
|---|---|
| Three-quarters rule | Insured if weekly scheduled hours and monthly scheduled days are three-quarters or more of a regular worker |
| Expanded coverage for short-hour workers | At a specified covered establishment with more than 51 insured persons on a constant basis, insured if weekly scheduled hours are 20 or more, monthly wage is 88,000 yen or more, employment is expected for over 2 months, and the person is not a student (expanded to 51+ employees from October 2024) |

The idea of social insurance for short-hour workers is in [Social insurance for short-hour workers and the three-quarters rule](/en/labor/column/tanjikan-koyo-shakaihoken-4bunno3), and the course of expanded coverage in [How far will the expansion of social-insurance coverage go](/en/labor/column/shakaihoken-tekiyo-kakudai-2027). Judging the insured status of a part-timer whose hours rise only in long holidays can differ where the contractual scheduled hours and the reality diverge (**unverified**).

## How does the labour of after-school day services' staffing standard differ?

An after-school children's club (gakudo) and an after-school day service have similar names but are different systems. Confusing them changes both the staffing standard and the governing law.

| Item | After-school children's club (gakudo) | After-school day service |
|---|---|---|
| System | After-school child health development service (Child Welfare Act Article 6-3(2)) | Ambulatory support for children with disabilities (Child Welfare Act Article 6-2-2) |
| Target | School-age children whose guardians are absent from home in the daytime due to work etc. | Children with disabilities attending school |
| Staffing basis | Ordinance No. 63 of 2014, Article 10 (two or more support staff per support unit) | Ordinance of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare No. 15 of 2012 (child instructor or nursery teacher, child development support manager, etc.) |
| Notification / designation | Notification to the municipality (Child Welfare Act Article 34-8) | Designation by the prefecture etc. |

The labour of after-school day services' staffing and full-time-equivalent conversion is in [After-school day services' comprehensive-support type / specific-programme type and staffing](/en/labor/column/houkago-day-jihatsu-sougou-shien-jido-shidouin-haichi) and [Staffing standards and labour for child development support and after-school day services](/en/labor/column/jido-hattatsu-houkago-day-jinin-kijun-roumu). The way of counting the full-time equivalent itself is in [How do you calculate the full-time equivalent](/en/labor/column/shogai-fukushi-kaigo-jokin-kansan-keisan-jinin), and the staffing and treatment of a nursery in [Staffing standards and treatment when opening a nursery](/en/labor/column/hoikuen-kaisetsu-roumu-haichi-kijun-shogu). ## Frequently asked questions

**Q. How many after-school child support staff should we place?**
A. Article 10 of the Standards on Equipment and Operation of After-School Child Health Development Services (Ordinance No. 63 of 2014) sets two or more after-school child support staff per support unit. However, all but one may be replaced by an assistant. A support unit is composed of approximately 40 or fewer children. This standard on number and qualification became a national "standard to refer to" from 1 April 2020, and the applicable standard is set by the municipality's ordinance, so check with the municipality you notify.

**Q. What qualification is needed to be a support worker?**
A. A person who holds a basic qualification such as nursery teacher or certified social worker and has completed training conducted by the prefectural governor etc. becomes an after-school child support worker (Ordinance No. 63 of 2014, Article 10(3)). An assistant has no such qualification requirement and assists the support staff. A revision of the training-eligibility requirements is planned; confirm the latest content with the prefecture/municipality's guidance (**unverified**).

**Q. What shifts work for the long opening hours?**
A. Because the club opens from after school into the evening on weekdays and from morning into the evening in long holidays, one worker's scheduled hours cannot cover it. Use an early/late rotation that always secures two or more per support unit. Where weekdays and long holidays differ greatly, a monthly (Labour Standards Act Article 32-2) or yearly (Article 32-4) variable working-hours system is an option. The yearly system cannot use the 44-hour-a-week special rule and calculates on 40 hours a week.

**Q. When do part-time support staff need social insurance?**
A. If weekly scheduled hours and monthly scheduled days are three-quarters or more of a regular worker, they become insured under health and employees' pension insurance. Even below three-quarters, they join if, at a specified covered establishment with more than 51 insured persons on a constant basis, they meet 20+ weekly hours, a monthly wage of 88,000 yen or more, employment expected over 2 months, and not being a student (expanded to 51+ employees from October 2024). Judgement where hours rise only in long holidays differs by individual circumstances.
