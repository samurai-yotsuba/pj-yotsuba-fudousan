**In short:** Working conditions of employees you take over in a business succession or M&A are in principle carried over as they are. When you "unify" wages, working hours and retirement allowances into the buyer's system, changing them to the employees' disadvantage is in principle by individual agreement (Labor Contract Act Article 9). To lower them by changing the work rules without agreement, you must make the changed work rules known and the change must be reasonable (Article 10). Reasonableness is judged by the degree of disadvantage the workers bear, the necessity of the change, the appropriateness of the changed content, and the state of labour-management negotiation. From a Shakai Hoken Roumushi's view, this article sets out the unification of work rules and the design of disadvantageous change, and separates collective labour disputes to an attorney, registration to a judicial scrivener, and tax on retirement allowances to a tax accountant.

"We merged, but the two companies have different wage tables and retirement allowances," and "We want to align a part-timer taken over in a business transfer to our work rules" — these are questions we often hear from HR and management after a succession is done. This page focuses not on the succession procedure itself but on how to unify working conditions afterwards.

## Must you keep the working conditions of taken-over employees as they are?

How the labour contract transfers changes with the succession method. First confirm which method your succession used to take over employees.

| Succession method | Transfer of labour contracts | Point |
|---|---|---|
| Merger | Universal succession. Labour contracts pass as they are to the surviving/new company | No individual consent needed. Working conditions are in principle carried over |
| Company split | Under the Labour Contract Succession Act, the contract of a worker mainly engaged in the succeeded business passes in principle | There are statutory procedures such as notice to and consultation with workers |
| Business transfer | Specific succession. Transfer of a labour contract needs each worker's consent in principle | Transfer needs the person's consent; conditions are set/agreed anew at the transferee |
| Share transfer | The employer (company) does not change | Labour contracts stay; the issue arises when changing work rules afterwards |

In every method, **the working conditions at the moment of takeover are in principle kept as they are** as the starting point. To lower them to fit the buyer's system requires, as below, agreement or a reasonable change of work rules. For the labour procedure of the succession itself, see [how do you carry over labour and social insurance in a business succession or M&A](/en/labor/column/jigyo-shokei-ma-roumu-shakaihoken); for succession of labour contracts on a company split, see [how are labour contracts succeeded in a company split or business transfer](/en/labor/column/kaisha-bunkatsu-jigyo-joto-roudou-keiyaku-shokei).

## Where is the line for "disadvantageous change" when unifying work rules?

Changing work rules in a direction that lowers working conditions raises the "disadvantageous change" issue. The line is drawn by whether the change is disadvantageous to the worker.

| Nature of change | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Change advantageous to workers (wage increase, more leave, etc.) | The reasonableness issue rarely arises. Changing/filing the work rules suffices |
| Disadvantageous change (cutting wages/retirement allowance, extending hours, etc.) | In principle individual agreement (Labor Contract Act Article 9). Without agreement, knowledge + reasonableness (Article 10) is needed |

Labor Contract Act Article 9 provides that **in principle an employer cannot change work rules to alter working conditions to workers' disadvantage without agreeing with the workers.** Article 10, however, provides that if the changed work rules are made known to the workers and the change is reasonable, the content of the changed work rules becomes the working conditions. In other words, a disadvantageous change must go through one of two routes: "individual agreement" or "knowledge + reasonableness." For the duty to create/file work rules themselves, see [why do work rules become mandatory at 10 or more people, and what do you write](/en/labor/column/shugyokisoku-10nin-gimu-nani-ga-hitsuyo).

## By what is the reasonableness of a change judged (wages, hours, retirement allowance)?

Labor Contract Act Article 10 lists the factors for judging the reasonableness of a change to work rules. This framework codifies the thinking built up in Supreme Court precedents on disadvantageous change of work rules (the Daishi Bank case, the Michinoku Bank case, etc.).

| Factor (Article 10) | What is looked at |
|---|---|
| Degree of disadvantage the workers bear | Width of the cut, number of people affected, impact on livelihood |
| Necessity of the change | Business necessity for unification. The more important the condition (wages, retirement allowance), the higher the necessity required |
| Appropriateness of the changed content | Presence of compensatory/transitional measures, comparison with the general social level |
| State of negotiation with a labour union, etc. | Consultation/agreement with a union or majority representative |
| Other circumstances | Making the change known, improvement of other conditions |

In particular, **wages and retirement allowances are treated as important working conditions**, and the precedents' thinking is that changing them to workers' disadvantage requires "reasonable content based on a high degree of necessity." Merely "the two companies have different systems, so we align them" hardly meets reasonableness; the key is whether you can explain, together, the necessity, the compensatory measures and the course of negotiation. The design of retirement allowances and post-retirement treatment also relates to [how do you design re-employment after mandatory retirement and securing work up to age 70](/en/labor/column/teinen-saikoyo-70sai-shugyo-kakuho). **Whether reasonableness exists is ultimately a matter for the courts to judge,** so design changes likely to be disputed carefully in advance.

## How do you design transitional measures and individual consent?

The practice when proceeding with a disadvantageous change is a combination of "taking individual consent carefully" and "placing transitional measures that raise reasonableness."

- **Individual consent:** Explain the content and degree of disadvantage concretely in writing and obtain consent based on free will. In precedent, consent to a disadvantageous change of important rights such as wages/retirement allowance is judged carefully by whether reasonable grounds objectively exist to recognise it as based on free will with full understanding of the effect. A design that does not end with a mere seal is needed
- **Transitional measures (softening the shock):** Guaranteeing the former level for a period, paying the difference as an adjustment allowance, or shifting in stages raises the appropriateness (reasonableness) of the changed content
- **Labour-management consultation:** Record the course of consultation with a union or majority representative. It is itself a factor of Article 10
- **Making it known:** Actually make the changed work rules known to workers. Knowledge is a requirement of Article 10

Which of wages, retirement allowance and hours to unify, when and in what order, is designed to fit the reality after the succession. Trying to unify everything at once right after the succession makes the degree of disadvantage large and the reasonableness hard to explain. **A combination of staged unification and transitional measures** is the basic form for avoiding disputes.

## FAQ

**Q. We merged, so can we unify wages to the lower company's level?**
A. Unifying by lowering wages is a disadvantageous change and needs individual agreement (Labor Contract Act Article 9) or knowledge and reasonableness (Article 10). Wages are treated as an important condition and require reasonable content based on a high degree of necessity. Because merely "wanting to align" hardly meets reasonableness, design it with the necessity, compensatory measures and course of negotiation together.

**Q. If we get consent forms from everyone, may we cut retirement allowances?**
A. A change is possible with consent, but consent to a disadvantageous change of an important right such as a retirement allowance is judged carefully by whether it is objectively recognised as based on free will with full understanding. Explain the content and degree of disadvantage concretely in writing; a design that does not end with a mere seal is needed. If likely to be disputed, consult an attorney too.

**Q. Can we align a part-timer taken over in a business transfer to our work rules?**
A. Transfer of a labour contract in a business transfer needs the person's consent in principle, and conditions at the transferee are set/agreed anew. Lowering them by work rules after transfer raises the disadvantageous-change issue, needing agreement or reasonableness. Because the starting point differs by method, first confirm which method took them over.

**Q. Who judges whether a change of work rules is reasonable?**
A. It is judged by the factors of Labor Contract Act Article 10 (degree of disadvantage, necessity, appropriateness of content, state of labour-management negotiation, etc.), and ultimately it is a matter for the courts. A Shakai Hoken Roumushi handles the design of the change and the creation/filing of work rules; legal judgment on lawfulness and representation of a dispute are an attorney's work. Consult by dividing the roles.

- The thinking that requires a high degree of necessity for reasonableness of a disadvantageous change and for changes to important working conditions has been built up in Supreme Court precedents such as the Daishi Bank case (Supreme Court judgment of 28 February 1997) and the Michinoku Bank case (Supreme Court judgment of 7 September 2000), and is codified in Labor Contract Act Article 10. The reasonableness in an individual case and the validity of consent are matters the court judges on the facts, so this article does not give a conclusive answer and organises them as design points to note (unverified)
- The transfer of labour contracts by succession method (merger = universal succession, company split = succession under the Labour Contract Succession Act, business transfer = specific succession needing individual consent, share transfer = employer unchanged) is a general organisation of the system; individual application differs by case
